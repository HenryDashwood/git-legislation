# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Legal, policy, civic-tech, and developer users who need to inspect a reproducible local corpus of UK legislation. They are usually comparing source material, parsed text, metadata, and versions while doing research or building legislation data workflows.

## Product Purpose

The product makes legislation data browseable, inspectable, and explainable. Success means a user can quickly find a law, understand what metadata and version terms mean, read the parsed content, and compare it with the source material when confidence matters.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, trustworthy. The interface should feel like a careful research instrument rather than a flashy publication or generic dashboard.

## Anti-references

Avoid decorative legal clichés, dense unexplained database dumps, flashy gradients, opaque abbreviations, and UI that hides uncertainty about parsed or PDF-backed records.

## Design Principles

- Explain the corpus as users browse it; metadata labels and legislation type codes should teach rather than assume prior knowledge.
- Keep source confidence visible by pairing parsed content with canonical files, source URLs, and PDF alternatives where available.
- Prefer familiar product UI patterns over novelty, with restrained color and strong readability.
- Make filters explicit and reversible so users can narrow the corpus without losing context.
- Treat accessibility as part of trust: clear contrast, keyboard-friendly controls, readable line lengths, and reduced-motion-safe interactions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA contrast and interaction basics. The UI should work with keyboard navigation, avoid color-only meaning, keep motion minimal, and use plain language for legal/data terms where possible.
